# Environmentalist | October 13 2025

Onel5969, as he was known on X, sank into his sofa and sighed contentedly. He preferred moments like this, where he can feel the world quiet down and it was just him, alone. He was not particularly distinguished, but he was never bothered by that: he never felt a need to be any aspiring genius and lived namelessly but peacefully. After a long Friday, it was finally his favorite time of the week. As usual, he loaded up X on his phone, ready to catch up on what the world had been up to while he was at his 9 to 5\. Countless political propaganda and Tesla posts competed for his attention, and he duly satisfied the demand of each and every one of them. On this day however, there was a new contestant on his screen: a post from MaterialScientist.  
Onel5969 shifted in his seat. New post from him\! His last one about air pollution was absolutely eye-opening, but in this one. . . Onel5969’s eyes fell on the appalling image of a beach covered fully in trash, a majority of which are bottles, cups, and various other single-use items. Shocked, Onel5969 slowly scrolled back up to read the accompanying text: “@amazon if your factories cut down on producing things people don’t really need, maybe we would still have this beach.”  
Wow, thought Onel5969. Living in the midst of NYC and never being a beach person, he could barely bring to mind a beach, let alone imagine one filled with human-produced plastic. To be clear, he obviously knew that humans are doing a lot of harm to the environment, but something concrete, like this image, hit differently than that abstract concept. He just cannot reconcile the image of the magnificent city he lived in with this beach on some corner of the planet, trashed by Amazon’s unsustainable production goals.  
Onel5969 was not one to repost, but he felt it necessary to express his shock. “First time seeing first-hand the damage we are doing,” he wrote in response to MaterialScientist. “We really need to try harder to protect our planet.”

Oliver had been to Jason’s apartment often. It was a very nice apartment in his opinion: tall, wooden closets that smell almost like antiques; a picture wall capturing Jason’s life; and warm, soothing, yellow lights at night. It was clearly a very intimate space for Jason, so Oliver was glad he was allowed to be a part of it.   
Jason, on the other hand, was an unconventional, secret genius. At the very least, he should be equal to any CEO of Wall Street. Every month for years he managed, with brilliant financial decisions, to consistently save at least fifty dollars by shopping Amazon sales and obtaining elusive coupons. His purchases ranged from a dinosaur costume that was 50% off (too bad it turned out too big), to a \$200 professional frisbee golf set that was on sale for \$60 (too bad NYC only has one disc golf course that he never went to). His talent in finance went hand in hand to his talent in storage, as he had constant opportunities to practice the art of Tetris in his closets. For almost twelve years he had lived happily in this apartment, but now that he was moving out, his genius turned against him with all its might.  
Sighing, Jason looked around at his apartment, which was already looking—but far from—empty now that he had packed one box. His back was already complaining. The sunshine of Saturday noon warmly embraced the packing boxes he had laid out, almost sympathizing with the heavy weight each of them was going to bear. He needed a helper.   
Half an hour later, Oliver entered the apartment. Jason did not move often, and Oliver cannot even remember if he had even known Jason the last time he had moved. However, he was now certain that he had not, since he did not expect those tall, wooden closets to be filled with Jason’s “good deals”, not to mention the fact that all four of them are filled floor to ceiling\! Really Jason’s creativity should have been applauded, since Oliver had never seen so diverse a collection inside anyone’s closet.  
“Why on Earth do you have four closets full of . . .” Oliver struggled for a word to describe the breathtaking scene. Not bothering to hide the frustration in his voice now that he realized what he got into, he blurted out, “I thought those were for clothes\!”  
“These are good deals\!” protested Jason frowning.  
“Good deals?” Oliver repeated. “Why do you have a dinosaur costume? And a frisbee bag and everything? Not once have I seen you—”  
“You never know\! I could use these\!” Jason retorted. “One day—maybe when I’m retired—I’ll learn to throw a frisbee, and then it will come in very handy\! As for the dinosaur costume, I can wear it to the next party I go to.”  
Incredulous, Oliver shook his head and started stuffing the two-meter-tall costume into one of the boxes without bothering to utter another word to his friend.

If I had to name a second favorite time of the week, it had to be Saturday night, thought Onel5969 as he made himself comfortable on his sofa. He shut his eyes for a moment, but opened them again to check his phone. A new post from MaterialScientist. Already? He usually doesn’t post this often\! Onel5969 let himself be redirected to the post by the notification. This one was just a line of text. “Everyone knows how to reduce carbon emissions. Yet at least half of U.S. workers still drive to work alone,” he read.  
Driving to work? On this issue Onel5969 felt the need to defend his daily routine. It’s so much more convenient, and you get so much more freedom\! If I—hypothetically since I’ve actually never done that—wanted to stop by Starbucks, I could do that if I’m in a car, but that would be such a hassle if I went by metro, not to mention the condition of metro stations here. Who would actually take the metro in NYC?  
Thus justified, Onel5969 easily dismissed the accusation and simply scrolled on.

Jason was turning off the lights in the living room when he was struck again by the emptiness of the apartment. They had managed to clean out two of the four closets in the afternoon, and he was more ready than ever to have some downtime, also known as Netflix time. Oliver had suggested to him the *Our Planet* series in passing, mentioning that “it’s a fitting show for you”. The title had really appealed to Jason. It seemed like a suspenseful title for a global drama, so he had a bag of popcorn ready as he sat down on the couch.  
Suffice it to say, the show was not what he expected. However, he did enjoy it. That’s an understatement. He found it absolutely fascinating. Never being someone to travel and having rarely seen the ocean, the show had many surprises for him, and he was in awe when he saw billions of locusts travel from Africa all the way to Tibet. It was an experience so novel to him that he could even overlook Oliver’s sarcasm and actually enjoy the show.  
But the episode soon took on a dark tone as Jason was marveling at the albatrosses on the island. “But what looks like a lifeline . . . could be a death sentence,” lamented the narrator as the documentary depicted a chick dying from ingesting plastic. Jason’s eyes widened. Animals dying on remote islands? As the narrator continued to explain the scale of the issue, it sounded almost surreal to him, as he knew neither the magnitude nor the severity of it. In the back of his mind, he replayed the afternoon when Oliver condemned him for contributing to environmental pollution with his pointless orders. This cannot be my fault\! He protested. I invest cleverly, while others just buy things for the thrill of it and throw them away soon after, and those are the people causing the degradation of our Earth\! How could anyone think *I’m* in the wrong\! Thus justified, Jason easily dismissed the guilt and simply watched on.

The next morning, hard-working Jason started on the packing of the third closet. However, his work was soon interrupted by the doorbell. Phew, reinforcement is here. Jason got up relieved and opened the door. “Hi—” started him, but froze when he saw the angry expression on Oliver’s face.  
“Hypocrite\!” accused Oliver indignantly.  
Jason’s confusion was not inconsiderable. Huh?? What could that possibly be for? His expression turned into one of confusion and annoyance.  
“You\! Out of everyone, you\!” Oliver continued yelling. “You want to deceive everyone on who you are, don’t you? No one would’ve found out if it weren’t for me\!”  
“What the hell are you talking about?” Jason cannot contain his anger anymore. “You come storming through the hallway, and yell loud enough to spread information that will probably take months to erase from my neighbors’ memory. Will I have the honor to know what you are here for?”  
“How do you not have any idea?” scolded Oliver incredulously. “Your X post\! I saw it last night. If I hadn’t checked X on a whim, you would have probably gone unnoticed with your ‘we must try harder to protect our planet’ all the while wasting money on useless things\! You would be the last person qualified to condemn Amazon for producing too much. Do you really think we need to protect our planet?”  
“Yes\!” Jason answered without hesitation.  
“Then why do you do this?” Oliver snapped and pointed at the remaining two closets, still full of all kinds of items.  
Jason was speechless for a split second. He had not expected such a reaction from Oliver at all, and it made him feel extremely lonely. No one understood how much work he put into the search for discounts, Amazon coupons, and everything in between. It’s really difficult\! Not to mention all of his considerations behind each and every order. At any rate, he was definitely not polluting the Earth: he threw away few of his purchases, so he felt responsible for none of the plastic floating on the ocean.  
Oliver wouldn’t understand any of this though.  
“Just go back to your place,” Jason sighed. “I’ll pack on my own.” And with that, he shut the door.  
The hallway fell silent, and Jason quietly returned to packing feeling like a misunderstood genius.

By that night, all but one of the closets had been cleared out. Jason, also known on X as Onel5969, sank into his sofa and took a deep breath, enjoying his quiet apartment. He was not particularly distinguished, except perhaps at saving money on Amazon, which he still took pride in. As usual, he loaded up X and started scrolling. MaterialScientist had made another post, condemning overconsumerism for trashing the ocean. Jason nodded appreciatively. People really aren’t doing *anything* to reduce their environmental impact. They are destroying our home by being selfish and indifferent. Musingly, he shook his head, disappointed in human nature. If only people realize the importance of conserving the Earth.  
As for me, I’m *most* supportive of that, thought him.
